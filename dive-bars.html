The Haywood Road Dive Bar Crawl

Seven bars, one road, no car needed. Written by somebody who drives people to and from all of them.

Updated August 2026

Almost every good dive bar in Asheville sits on one road. Haywood Road runs the length of West Asheville, and over about a mile and a half of it you can hit seven bars on foot without ever needing to move your car. This is the run I describe to passengers roughly four nights a week, so here it is written down properly.

Why West Asheville and not downtown

Downtown has dive bars, and good ones. But downtown also has bachelorette parties, twenty-dollar cocktails, a parking deck problem, and a crowd that turns over completely every weekend. West Asheville is where the people who work in those downtown bars go when their shift ends. The drinks are cheaper, the rooms are smaller, and nobody is performing for anybody.

The practical advantage is the geography. Haywood Road is a straight line. The bars sit in two loose clusters — one around the 375–510 block, one around the 775–1045 block — with about a fifteen-minute walk between them. You can do the whole thing without driving, which is the entire point.

How I'd sequence it: Start at Desoto around 7 to eat something. Walk to Double Crown and Fleetwood's. Then either stop there, or push west to Westville and Universal Joint. The Odd is the late one — go there last, or don't go at all if you want a conversation.

The lower block — 375 to 510 Haywood

375 Haywood RdDouble Crown

If a passenger asks for one bar and one bar only, this is the answer. It's dim, red-lit, and small, with soul, country, and R&B 45s spinning most nights. The drinks are honest and not expensive. It has the trick that very few new bars manage, which is feeling like it has been there for fifty years when it hasn't.

It gets tight after about 10 on a weekend. If you want a seat and an actual conversation, go early. Parking on this block is street-only and it fills up, which is one more argument for getting dropped off.

496 Haywood RdFleetwood's

Vintage clothing shop, dive bar, and a functioning wedding chapel, all in the same building. This is not a bit — people really do get married here, sometimes late at night, sometimes in outfits they bought off the rack twenty feet away. There's a stage in the back and a steady run of punk, garage, and rock shows.

Come for the room even if the band isn't your thing. Hours run to midnight most weeknights and 2am on Friday, and they open at noon on weekends.

504 Haywood RdDesoto Lounge

The easiest room on the strip for a group, and the one I'd start at. Pinball, ping-pong, foosball, a jukebox worth feeding, local beer on tap, and a back patio most people don't notice from the street. The kitchen makes actual food and runs late — nachos, sandwiches, that category — which matters a lot more at 11pm than people expect.

Doors open at 11am on weekdays and 10 on weekends, and it stays open till 1 on Friday and Saturday. If somebody in your group isn't drinking, this is the bar that won't bore them.

507 Haywood RdThe Whale

Half a step up from a dive, and I include it anyway. It's a tiny beer bar with a list that turns over constantly — mostly Belgian and other imports you genuinely will not find elsewhere in town. If you've spent your whole trip drinking Asheville IPAs and want a break, this fixes it.

Small room, patio out front and back, and it draws a slightly quieter crowd than its neighbors. Good first or last stop, less good as the one where your group of eight settles in.

The upper block — 775 to 1045 Haywood

777 Haywood RdWestville Pub

The true neighborhood pub of the strip, and it's been on that corner for over twenty years. Pool tables, trivia nights, live music, cheap pints, big front windows for watching the road. They brew their own now under the All Sevens name, and it's poured alongside everything else.

Open 10:30 in the morning until 2am, every single day, which makes it the most reliable door on Haywood. If you show up in West Asheville with no plan and no idea what's open, walk here.

784 Haywood RdUniversal Joint

Directly across the street from Westville, which makes the two of them the natural hinge point of the crawl. Burgers, tater tots, fish tacos, and one of the largest patios in West Asheville. When the weather is decent, this is where the neighborhood ends up.

It's more bar-with-food than bar, which is useful when half your group wants dinner and the other half wants a fourth beer. Crossing Haywood here on a Friday night takes patience — there's traffic and not much of a crosswalk.

1045 Haywood RdThe Odd

Formerly the Odditorium, and still the most committed room on the road. Punk, metal, drag, burlesque, comedy, and murals covering every surface inside and out. Open until 2am seven nights a week.

This is not a quiet-conversation bar and it isn't trying to be. It's also the furthest west of the group, a real walk from the lower block, so plan on it being your last stop rather than a casual drop-in. If you're the kind of person who'd rather see a band you've never heard of than talk about your day, start here instead.

Practical things nobody tells you

  • Last call is 2am statewide. North Carolina cuts alcohol service at 2, and the bars that stay open latest — The Odd, Westville, Fleetwood's on Friday and Saturday — will all be pushing people out around the same time. That's the busiest pickup window of the night on Haywood.
  • Parking is the real problem. Haywood is street parking and small private lots, most of them signed for one business only. Tow trucks work this road. This is genuinely a stretch to get dropped off on rather than drive to.
  • The gap between clusters is about fifteen minutes on foot. Flat, sidewalked, well lit enough. Fine on a nice night, less fun in February rain — that's a short ride, not a walk.
  • Cash still helps. Cards work everywhere, but the smaller rooms move faster if you're not opening a tab for one drink, and bartenders on this road are working for tips.
  • Hours shift. Kitchens close earlier than bars, and small bars change their schedules more than chains do. If a specific place is the whole plan, call first.

Getting there and getting home

From downtown Asheville the lower block is about a ten-minute ride, a little more on a Friday when the bridge backs up. From the airport it's roughly twenty-five minutes. From the River Arts District you're close enough that people try to walk it and then regret the hill.

The honest advice is to get dropped at Desoto or Double Crown, walk the strip, and get picked up wherever you end up. Between the parking situation, the two-lane traffic, and the fact that the entire appeal of this road is that you can drink on it, driving yourself here makes very little sense.
